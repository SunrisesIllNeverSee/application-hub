## Idea

### Why this idea? Domain expertise and need

My background in video games and statistics led me to notice that AI systems benchmark models but do not measure the operators using them. Existing measures make it difficult to distinguish useful human-AI work from token volume or performative usage. I built SigRank to measure operator yield and human-AI synchronization from language-derived signals while protecting private prompt content. That work led to Commitment Theory and the broader governance substrate, because systems need to measure whether meaning and commitments survive as agents transform language. Early evidence includes 1,627 seeded operators, 40+ active users, published research, public artifacts, and live product surfaces. The next test is whether a defined group of operators, employers, or teams will repeatedly use and pay for this standard.

### Competitors and insight

Adjacent competitors include AI observability and evaluation tools such as LangSmith, Braintrust, and Langfuse. They log prompts, traces, or model outputs and primarily measure the system or model output. MO§ES starts with the operator: how a human directs an AI system, what signal survives transformation, and whether the resulting action remains aligned with the original commitment. AI performance is a relationship between a system and an operator, not a property of the model alone.

### Monetization

Paid operator profiles and performance features for AI-native users; team and enterprise measurement; a Commitment Quality Monitor for teams building AI agents; and licensing governed-execution infrastructure to regulated enterprises. The near-term commercial test is a paid design-partner product for teams that need to measure operator performance or preserve meaning and evidence across agent workflows.

### Other ideas

Signomy, an agentic-economy marketplace with dual governance (signomy.xyz); AQUA (mos2es.xyz), an application operating system that turns recurring questions and answers into a reusable bank; SigRank; and the Post-Turing Test, a measurement standard for human-AI collaboration depth.
