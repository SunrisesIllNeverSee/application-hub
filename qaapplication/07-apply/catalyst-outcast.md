## Q → A

### Where are you in your founder journey?

I am a solo founder with a live product wedge, an operating history, and a clear view of the kind of company I want to build—but I am actively looking for the right technical co-founder before committing the next several years of my life to a company together. I have built and run a 40-person operating business, and over the past year I taught myself to build AI-native products with modern coding tools. The product is now live, with 1,628 seeded operators and 40+ users on its leaderboard. ### What have you done that’s genuinely impressive?

I built and ran a restoration and renovation company in Buffalo for 15 years, growing it from zero to roughly $1M in annual revenue and a 40-person team. That taught me the unglamorous parts of building: hiring, accountability, cash flow, customer trust, and making decisions when the answer is not obvious.

Then, without a software background, I started working deeply with AI in July 2025. In roughly a year, I built and shipped multiple live products, including an operator-performance product with 1,628 seeded operators and 40+ users on its live leaderboard. I also published a falsifiable research framework and built the systems around it myself. The accomplishment I value most is not a metric—it is proving to myself that I can enter a new field, learn quickly, and turn an insight into something real.

### What’s the riskiest decision you’ve made? Why did you make it?

Starting over in AI after spending 15 years building a company in an entirely different industry was the riskiest decision I have made. I had no computer science pedigree and no existing technical network. But I recognized that AI was becoming a new operating layer for how companies work, and I did not want to spend the next decade watching from the sidelines.

I made the decision because I trust my ability to learn through building. I was not betting on a credential; I was betting on curiosity, endurance, and the pattern recognition I developed from running a real business. The risk was real: time, money, and reputation. But it gave me a second domain where I now have conviction and evidence.

### What’s the first thing you ever built or sold?

The first substantial thing I built and sold was my restoration and renovation business. I started with work I could personally deliver, earned trust job by job, and grew it into a roughly 40-person operation. It taught me that selling is not persuasion theater—it is making a promise, delivering well, and earning the right to be trusted again.

### What's something weird or niche you were obsessed with? How deep did you go?

I have long been obsessed with video-game systems: rankings, progression loops, hidden mechanics, and the difference between someone who plays a game and someone who understands its underlying economy. I went deep enough that it shaped how I think about work.

When AI tools appeared, I noticed that everyone benchmarked models but almost nobody measured the human operator using them live. That led me to build an operator-performance product. The original instinct was almost embarrassingly simple: if people spend meaningful time with AI, there should be a way to understand whether they are actually getting better at using it. The resulting field analysis exposed something I found deeply satisfying: high-yield operators can be nearly invisible on volume leaderboards. Volume is activity; yield is performance.
